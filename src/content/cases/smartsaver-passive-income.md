<b>Business wanted fewer withdrawals. So I proposed automating them.</b>

Sounds a bit backwards, doesn't it? Yet that idea became <b>Passive Income</b>, a feature I introduced at Monefit SmartSaver after spotting an unusual habit in our transaction data.

<b>My role:</b> Senior Product Manager. I spotted the opportunity, worked with analysts to investigate it, defined the product and led the work with UX and engineering through launch.

## The strange pattern

SmartSaver earns money for investors while their capital helps fund Creditstar's lending business. Naturally, we wanted people to keep more of their money invested.

While looking into withdrawals, I noticed some customers doing the same thing every month. Their withdrawal amounts were suspiciously close to their monthly earnings. Some had kept up this routine for at least five months.

I asked our analysts to compare withdrawal amounts, earnings and frequency. The pattern held up. And in customer interviews, a few investors had mentioned enjoying similar automatic payouts elsewhere.

It made me wonder: <b>how many of these people actually wanted to withdraw their investment, and how many just wanted a monthly income?</b>

## So, why not automate it?

My hypothesis was simple: let customers receive their earned interest automatically every month, while keeping the invested capital in place.

I deliberately kept the first version small:

- <b>One opt-in toggle.</b> Customers could switch Passive Income on without configuring a complicated payout plan.
- <b>One monthly schedule.</b> It matched the rhythm of salaries, bills and household budgets.
- <b>A clear view of the next payout.</b> Estimated amount, date and payout method, all in one place.
- <b>A preview before activation.</b> The Summary page showed estimated monthly income even when Passive Income was switched off.

That last decision gave the feature a useful growth angle. Even someone earning just a few euros could see what their balance might pay out each month. And maybe start wondering what that number would look like with a larger investment.

## Did it work?

<b>Roughly 30% of investors adopted Passive Income.</b> Plenty of smaller investors enabled it too, sometimes for payouts worth only a few euros or cents. Apparently, seeing money arrive regularly is satisfying regardless of the amount.

Among customers who enabled the feature, we observed <b>fewer manual withdrawals on average</b> and a <b>slight increase in deposits</b>. The behavior was encouraging and matched the problem we had set out to solve.

Marketing also gained an easy-to-explain benefit: monthly income from your investment. CRM could connect that promise to the estimated earnings customers already saw on their Summary page.

## Why I'm proud of this one

The best part happened before anyone started designing screens. We noticed people doing something repeatedly, took the time to understand why and built a feature around their actual behavior.

And yes, it still amuses me that our idea for reducing manual withdrawals was to make withdrawals automatic.
