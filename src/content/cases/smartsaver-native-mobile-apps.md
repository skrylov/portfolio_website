<b>In January, SmartSaver had no native apps. We didn't even have a mobile team. By September, both Android and iOS were live.</b>

The tricky decision was knowing when to stop building and launch. Investors had been asking for an app for years. We wanted to deliver one before the autumn investment peak, while leaving enough time to make sure people's money and financial data were handled properly.

<b>My role:</b> Senior Product Manager. I led product scope and delivery with a newly assembled team of six mobile developers, working across UX, backend, QA and Legal. I also owned testing coordination and the Android/iOS publication process.

## Building two apps from zero

We recruited six developers, roughly half of them remote, and started turning an established web investment product into a native mobile experience.

Customers needed to check their balance, deposit money, manage Vaults and see their transactions. Behind those screens were the same financial rules and data already used on web. We had to make everything consistent across three platforms while keeping the web product running.

The new interface also gave us a chance to simplify the everyday experience. We wanted something people could open, understand and use without thinking too much about it.

## The difficult choice: what could wait?

We could have spent another month building everything customers already had on web. I chose to focus the first release on <b>existing investors and their most frequent tasks</b>.

- <b>Ship:</b> balances, deposits, Vaults, transactions and investment features such as Passive Income.
- <b>Defer:</b> new-customer onboarding and the full profile/settings experience. Customers could still register on web.
- <b>Keep the quality bar:</b> correct financial figures, no known critical bugs, and a usable experience on both platforms.

That scope decision was crucial. SmartSaver's investment activity typically peaked in autumn. Every extra week of polishing meant less time to reach customers during the busiest season.

## Everything around the apps

The mobile screens were only part of my job. I also had to coordinate:

- <b>Backend and APIs:</b> making existing financial workflows behave consistently on mobile and web.
- <b>CRM and push notifications:</b> adapting to a provider change during development.
- <b>Campaign banners:</b> bringing our <mention url="https://app.notion.com/p/4053732f32cc811eaa3af0d5b177eca6" /> into the apps from day one.
- <b>App stores and compliance:</b> preparing listings, privacy disclosures, financial-service explanations and reviewer access with Legal.

We tested with internal groups and around <b>100 customers and influencers</b>. By late August, the critical bugs were resolved and the financial figures matched web. We also added analytics instrumentation around launch so we could learn from actual usage.

So we went live. Android launched in early September 2026. iOS followed roughly two to three weeks later after a longer review process.

## Did the timing pay off?

We had forecast <b>30% mobile adoption by the end of 2026</b>. Roughly 5 weeks after launch, our internal adoption figure had already passed <b>50%</b>.

Both apps reached ratings of approximately <b>4.7/5</b>. Reviews highlighted simplicity, clarity and having the essentials available.

We also kept building. The profile and settings area, including biometrics and notification preferences, was in QA when I left. It hadn't been released yet.

## What I'm proud of

Starting a mobile product from scratch is demanding enough. Doing it with a brand-new team, an existing financial backend, two app-store review processes and a busy investment season approaching made it particularly interesting.

I still think launching with a focused scope was the right decision. We had solved the important problems, the numbers were right and customers were ready. Their adoption came much faster than we'd expected.
